Three things I'd change

1

Take bespoke orders online

What I found

Your FAQ answers "How do I order" with "You have the option to call or email Head Office", and says you get the fit right by "asking detailed questions, taking accurate measurements".

Why it matters

Each bespoke order means a call or a string of emails to collect measurements and choices, and people browse in the evening when the office is closed.

How it would work
  1. They pick the style, enter their measurements from your size guide, their height and fabric choice, and add a photo if it helps.
  2. Where you have a set price, such as trouser alterations from £12, they see it and pay there and then.
  3. You get one complete order with everything you need, and they get a message with your 3 to 4 week timescale.
2

Take pre-orders with payment

What I found

Your contact page says "For all enquires and pre-orders and bespoke items, please fill out the form below", and "One of our team will be in touch within 24 hours."

Why it matters

A pre-order sent through the contact form is only a note of interest until you've chased payment, and some go quiet in between.

How it would work
  1. A Pre-order button sits on the product page when a piece is coming back.
  2. They pay there and then, and get a confirmation by email.
  3. When the piece is ready, a message goes out by itself, and you see who is waiting for what.
3

Hold retreat places with a deposit

What I found

Your Retreat 2027 page says "Numbers will be limited if you would like further information sign up below."

Why it matters

With limited places, a list of names isn't the same as bookings. A deposit turns interest into firm places and tells you your numbers early.

How it would work
  1. People who signed up get first access to a booking page with the dates and price.
  2. They pay a deposit to hold a place, and a reminder for the balance goes out by itself.
  3. Once it's full, a waiting list fills any place that's given up.
